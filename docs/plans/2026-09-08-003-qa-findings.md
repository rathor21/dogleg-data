## 1. Throttled performance

Measured with CDP `Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate`, frame time from `requestAnimationFrame` timestamp deltas (a wrapper installed via `page.evaluateOnNewDocument` before navigation, so it captures every frame from the first tick). `headless: "new"` did not starve rAF on this machine; frame counts landed in the 820-825 range for every run, consistent with the animation's own ~2.2s flight time times five staggered shots plus settle.

| Viewport | CPU rate | Avg frame (ms) | p95 frame (ms) | First-30 avg (ms) | Degrade path | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1440x900 | 4x | 8.73 | 16.70 | 18.33 | not engaged | pass |
| 375x812 | 4x | 8.67 | 16.70 | 16.67 | not engaged | pass |
| 1440x900 | 6x | 8.77 | 16.70 | 19.44 | not engaged | pass |
| 375x812 | 6x | 8.71 | 16.70 | 17.78 | not engaged | pass |

All four pass (p95 under the 33ms bar). A control test confirmed CPU throttling applies in this environment (a busy loop ran 114.8ms unthrottled, 450.7ms at rate 4, 672.7ms at rate 6, scaling as expected), so the flat frame times above are a real result, not an inactive throttle. The hero's Canvas 2D draw work per frame sits far enough under the 16.7ms display-refresh budget that even a 6x CPU slowdown doesn't push it into jank. p95 clamps at 16.70ms in every configuration, consistent with headless Chrome pacing frames to a 60Hz compositor tick when the per-frame work fits inside it with room to spare.

The animation's own slow-frame degrade check (first 30 frames averaging over 40ms triggers a fallback to the settled render) never engaged at either throttle rate; the highest first-30 average recorded was 19.44ms, under half the 40ms trigger.

## 4. Capture card at native size

Changed `chapters.css` and `chapters.js` so `.capture-card` renders at true 1080x1350 CSS px instead of 420px CSS px later upscaled by `export.mjs`. Title 56px, key sentence (verdict or caption line) 34px, footer 22px, each sized for the real output canvas. The internal figure canvas resolution moved from 640 to 968px (the card's own width minus its 56px side padding), so every chart renders at 1:1 pixel density or finer at the export's `deviceScaleFactor: 1`, never upscaled into blur the way the old 640px canvas inside a 420px card, itself later upscaled 2.6x, was.

Two bugs surfaced and got fixed while wiring this up, both in `chapters.css`:

1. **Leaked chapters in capture mode.** The old hide rule (`body.capture-mode .chapters-root > .chapter:not(.is-captured)`) reached only the chapters nested inside `.chapters-root` (chapters 1-7). Chapters 9 (`#try-your-own-aim`) and 10 (`#method-and-sources`) sit outside that wrapper as later siblings and were never hidden, so a captured page would show the CTA card and the full sources section below whatever chapter was meant to stand alone. Replaced with one `body.capture-mode .chapter:not(.is-captured)` rule that reaches every chapter regardless of nesting.
2. **Card width collapsing under flex centering.** An initial attempt to center the card on a cream page used `body.capture-mode{display:flex;align-items:center;justify-content:center;}`. That shrank the card's own explicit `width:1080px` down to about 748px: a flex item with both a definite width and an aspect-ratio, in a container whose cross-axis alignment isn't `stretch`, does not keep that width in this browser. Moved to plain block flow (`margin:0 auto` on the card, no flex/grid on the body), which centers the card with no such interaction. Confirmed at 1080x1350.

One more bug found and fixed in `export.mjs`: `findCardElement`'s selector fallback list (`.capture-card, .chapter-card, .chapter-figure`) matched whichever `.chapter-figure` happened to render first in document order for chapters with no step-driven figure of their own (chapters 6 and 8 have no `.step` elements, so `chapters.js` never enters capture mode for them). The script then force-resized that wrong chapter's figure up to 1080x1350 and shipped it as chapter 6's or chapter 8's card. Fixed by checking `document.body.classList.contains("capture-mode")` before accepting any selector match; those two chapters now fall through to their `standinUrl` in `cards.json` instead.

`export.mjs` also now records the hero video at `deviceScaleFactor: 2` (was 1): the hero canvas's backing store sizes to `cssWidth * devicePixelRatio` (`hero.js`), and at dpr 1 that canvas is only about 1132px wide, the entire source for the portrait cut's 1080px-wide crop, hence the softening this pass was asked to check. At dpr 2 the source measures about 2264px wide, well above the 1920px floor.

## 5. Degrade path

Confirmed with CDP `Emulation.setEmulatedMedia({features: [{name: "prefers-reduced-motion", value: "reduce"}]})`: `window.__phaseComplete` reads `true` right after load, `window.__heroState` reports `{phase: "settled", shotsLanded: 5}`, and an injected `requestAnimationFrame` counter recorded zero calls. ## 6. Registration script

`art/registration_qa.py` is retired rather than retargeted, the smaller correct change. It measures a candidate against `sketch.py`'s stencil-camera coordinates (`sketch_coords.json`), the camera every round used through round five. Round six replaced that whole pipeline with a TPS camera fitted to the accepted painting itself (`art/fit_tps.py`), and `hero.png` as shipped is `r6_4` under that TPS fit, not anything the stencil camera describes. The TPS fit already records its own leave-one-out residual table in `art/README.md` ("Round six, camera correction" -> "Leave-one-out residuals (real landmarks alone, lambda=60)"), which is the real, current registration record for the shipped art. Retargeting `registration_qa.py` to reimplement TPS projection and residual reporting would duplicate a table that already exists, for no new information.

Added a retirement note to `art/README.md` (right after the Status line, most visible) pointing at that residual table, and a docstring warning at the top of `registration_qa.py`. Neither the script nor `sketch_coords.json` was deleted -- both stay for the rounds one through five historical record, per this repo's own convention of keeping earlier-round artifacts on file.

### 3. Hero pixel checks (new this pass)

The first pass could not judge the hero since another agent was still fixing its arcs and camera. This pass checks the shipped photoreal art and its shape-affine projection at the pixel level, in the settled state (`?settled=1`), desktop (1440x900) and phone (375x812).

Method: `window.__heroDebug.samplePath(shotId, 1)` gives each shot's landing point in the exact screen coordinates `hero.js`'s own `arcPoint`/`toScreen` compute. Pins are not shots, so their screen coordinates came from a separate re-implementation of `project()`/`toScreen()` fed by `camera_hero.json` and the live page's own `cssW`/`cssH`/`isMobile` (read from the DOM, not guessed): a plain restatement of the documented formulas, not a change to `hero.js`. Each point was then sampled against the **base art layer alone**, redrawn fresh on an offscreen canvas at 1:1 scale: sampling the composited on-screen canvas instead returns the drawn pin marker or landing dot's ink color, not the terrain underneath it, since the page paints those markers on top of the projected points by design.

| Pin, center | - | (153, 171, 88) | (153, 171, 87) | green turf |
| Pin, sunday | - | (132, 156, 77) | (132, 156, 77) | green turf |

### 4. Sandbox spot checks against Python

Ran `export.sandbox_lookup` from `analysis/003-augusta-12/` and compared against the page's own `window.__sandbox` debug hooks (`setTier`, `setPin`, `setWind`, `setCarry`, `setLateral`, `getLookup`) at 1440x900, reading all four KPI cards (score, water, short, green) plus the delta card.

| Case | Tier / pin / wind / lateral / carry | Page score | Page water | Page short | Page green | Python score / water / short / green |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 15 / sunday / off / 0 / 163yd (carryAdj 0.5, see note) | 4.19 | 16% | 30% | 21% | 4.1947 / 0.1586 / 0.2997 / 0.21375 |
| B | 20 / center / on / -3 / 159yd (carryAdj 4) | 4.21 | 11% | 39% | 15% | 4.2057 / 0.1134 / 0.3893 / 0.1451 |
| C | 0 / left / off / 12 / 138yd (carryAdj -10) | 3.80 | 20% | 43% | 26% | 3.8038 / 0.2042 / 0.426 / 0.2557 |
| D | 20 / center / off / 0 / 200yd (carryAdj 45) | 4.30 | 5% | 5% | 14% | 4.2986 / 0.0472 / 0.0543 / 0.1445 |

Cases B, C, and D match the Python output to the digit, on every one of the four KPI cards. Case A's target was the case named in the QA brief (`sandbox_lookup(g, 15, 'sunday', False, 0, 0)`, carry adjustment 0), but the carry input is an `<input type="range" step="1">`: setting it to 162.5 through `__sandbox.setCarry` (the pin's own y, giving carryAdj 0) reads back as 163 once the browser's range-input value sanitization snaps it, landing on carryAdj 0.5 instead. Re-ran Python at carryAdj 0.5 and it matches the page (score 4.1947, water 0.1586, short 0.2997, green 0.21375), so this is the range input's own integer step, not a sandbox bug; case A above reports the value the page reached.

Case D, the 200-yard-carry/20-handicap scenario the brief asked for by name: pin center, wind off, lateral 0 (the sandbox's own defaults), water 5% and short 5%. Both drop hard from the sandbox's other cases, as expected for a shot that carries 45 yards past the green: at that depth the model has the ball long, past the water and past the front-third short-game band, missing both rather than finding either one.

**Sync check.** Reset to tier 15 / center / off / lateral 0, then dragged the aim marker +40 client px to the right on the SVG map: the numeric field updated to lateral 5 (rightward drag increases lateral, matching the "negative is left, positive is right" label), and `getLookup()` recomputed to match a lateral-5 lookup. Then reset lateral to 0 and typed 15 into the numeric field: the marker's SVG transform moved from `translate(260.0, 401.8)` to `translate(378.2, 401.8)`, the same `sx(15)` value the map's own scale predicts, and `getLookup()` matched Python at lateral 15, carryAdj 0 to the digit (score 4.1359, water 0.1694, short 0.4767, green 0.1213). Both directions of the drag/field sync hold.
