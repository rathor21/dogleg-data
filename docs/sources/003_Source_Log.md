## Anchor 1 — Approach proximity by handicap, ~150-160 yards

**Status: PUBLISHED at handicap resolution, but scattered across single-point quotes rather than one clean table. Distance band matches the shot in question closely enough to use directly for at least two tiers; other tiers need interpolation.**

**Source 1:** Arccos Golf, ["Best 3 Golf Approach Stats To Look At For Faster Improvement"](https://eu.arccosgolf.com/blogs/community/top-3-approach-stats-to-know-about-your-golf-game). Retrieved 2026-08-13, full text loaded. States, in text (not an image): a 10-handicap golfer hits 43% of greens from 125-149 yards and finishes an average of 62 feet from the pin at that distance. This is a real, directly quoted Arccos number in the exact yardage band the 12th hole's tee shot falls into.

**Source 2:** Arccos Golf, ["Your Chances of Hitting The Green Might Be Lower Than You Think!"](https://www.arccosgolf.com/blogs/community/from-this-distance-you-have-a-50-chance-of-hitting-the-green). Retrieved 2026-08-13, full text loaded. Publishes, as text, the yardage at which each handicap tier hits 50% of greens (Arccos calls this the "magic number"): scratch 165 yd, 5-handicap 147 yd, 10-handicap 129 yd, 15-handicap 110 yd, 20-handicap 92 yd. This is a genuine published anchor and it brackets the 12th hole's 155-yard tee shot for the low-handicap tiers (scratch sits almost exactly at the shot length; 5 and 10-handicap sit inside it), but the mid-to-high tiers (15, 20, 25, 30) are all shooting a shot meaningfully longer than their own 50%-GIR distance, i.e., a below-average-odds shot for them specifically. Framing note for model tasks: this "magic number" table gives distance-for-fixed-probability, not proximity-for-fixed-distance, so it inverts differently than Source 1's foot-proximity number.

**Source 3:** Golf Monthly, ["How Close To The Pin Do Amateur Golfers Hit Their Approach Shots?"](https://www.golfmonthly.com/features/how-close-to-the-pin-do-amateur-golfers-hit-their-approach-shots), citing Shot Scope data. Retrieved 2026-08-13 (article rendered mostly as navigation chrome on fetch; two figures survived in the visible text): a 10-handicap from 125-149 yards hits about 43% of greens and finishes about 62 feet from the pin — matching Source 1 almost exactly, a useful independent cross-check even though both trace to the Shot Scope/Arccos amateur-tracking ecosystem rather than being fully independent datasets. Same article states, for a 10-handicap hitting from 150+ yards in the fairway, misses finish short of pin-high 41% of the time and long of pin-high 11% of the time — a genuine published **short/long split** at almost exactly the target distance, for one tier.

**Source 4 (aggregate, no handicap split):** Shot Scope, ["Approach shots: average proximity to the hole"](https://shotscope.com/blog/practice-green/stats-and-data/approach-shots-average-proximity/) and ["How many greens should you hit from 150 yards?"](https://shotscope.com/blog/practice-green/stats-and-data/hit-greens-from-150-yards/). Retrieved 2026-08-13. Text-readable figures: from 150 yards, the average golfer (all handicaps pooled) finishes almost 90 feet from the pin; from 130-150 yards, GIR% across all golfers is 38.64%. Both blog posts reference four additional charts (GIR by handicap, proximity by handicap) that render only as images; no numeric table extracted from them. Useful as a sanity-check aggregate, not as a per-tier anchor.

**Flag — inconsistent secondary compilation found and rejected as a primary anchor:** Golfity.com, ["Approach Proximity Benchmarks"](https://golfity.com/blog/approach-proximity-benchmarks-how-close-should-you-be/), gives a 150-yard table (Tour 25 ft, Scratch 29 ft, 10-handicap 42 ft, 20-handicap 62 ft) that cites no external data source in its own text. Its 20-handicap figure (62 ft) is identical to Arccos's directly-quoted 10-handicap figure at 125-149 yards (Source 1/3 above) — a strong sign this page relabeled or re-derived someone else's number rather than publishing an independent measurement. Not used as an anchor; noted here so no downstream task treats it as one.

**Verdict: ANCHORED** for scratch/5/10-handicap at the target yardage (Sources 1-3, Arccos and Shot Scope text, directly quoted, at or within 25 yards of 155). **MODELED (interpolated/extrapolated)** for 15/20/25/30-handicap, which have a "magic number" GIR-distance anchor (Source 2) but no directly-quoted foot-proximity figure at this specific yardage band — proximity for those tiers must be inferred from the GIR-distance curve, not read off a published table.

## Anchor 2 — Distance-vs-line anisotropy for irons

**Status: PUBLISHED, but the clean numeric ratio applies to a different distance band (short game/sand) than the 155-yard shot in question. For the 100-150-yard band itself, only a qualitative (non-numeric) statement is published.**

**Source:** Broadie, Mark (2008), ["Assessing Golfer Performance Using Golfmetrics,"](http://www.columbia.edu/~mnb2/broadie/Assets/broadie_wscg_v_200804.pdf) Chapter 34 in *Science and Golf V*. Same PDF 002 used for Anchors B and D. WebFetch's converter again returned only binary/compressed noise; text was extracted successfully with PyPDF2 (same workaround as 002). Full text confirmed and read this session.

The paper states explicitly, in its abstract and body: **"For amateur golfers, distance errors on short game and sand shots are shown to be about three times larger than direction errors."** This is a real, numeric, citable dist/dir ratio (~3:1), corroborated by Table 2's "Dist/dir" column for 20-60-yard shots: Pro1 1.5, Pro2 1.6, Am1 3.2, Am2 3.3, Am3 3.1 — i.e., the amateur groups (the relevant ones for this release) cluster tightly around 3:1 to 3.3:1, while pros run closer to 1.5:1-1.6:1.

**The catch:** this ratio is measured for 20-60-yard short-game shots and 0-50-yard sand shots (Figure 4), not for the 100-150-yard approach-iron band the 12th hole's tee shot actually falls into. The paper does publish 50% dispersion ellipses for the 100-150-yard band too (Figure 5, Am1 and Am2 groups, tee/fairway/rough lies), but only describes them qualitatively — "the distance error for tee shots is less than fairway shots, indicating that there is a noticeable advantage to using a tee" — with no printed dist/dir number for that band. The 100-150-yard ellipses are visually flatter (more elongated in the distance axis) than the short-game ellipses per the paper's own figures, but "flatter" was not quantified in text.

**Verdict: MODELED.** No source found (Broadie, Stagner, TrackMan, or otherwise) publishes a numeric distance-vs-line dispersion ratio specifically for 100-160-yard approach irons. The 3:1 amateur ratio from short game/sand is the only citable numeric anchor and must be carried across distance bands as an assumption, not a measurement. **Sensitivity range: 2:1 to 3.5:1** — floored below 3:1 because the paper's own qualitative description implies tee/fairway approach shots may be somewhat less distance-dominated than sand/short-game shots (better, more repeatable contact at longer distances), and capped near the published 3.1-3.3:1 amateur short-game value as the upper bound, since nothing in the source suggests approach irons are *more* distance-dominated than short-game shots.

## Anchor 3 — Green-hit geometry inputs: Augusta 12 specifics

**Status: Qualitative hazard layout is PUBLISHED and highly consistent across independent sources. Precise green width/depth numbers are PUBLISHED but badly inconsistent across sources — flagged, not resolved.**

**Yardage (ANCHORED, consistent everywhere):** 155 yards from the Masters tee. Confirmed directly by PGA.com ("How to Play the 12th Hole at Augusta National," retrieved 2026-08-13) and independently by golfguidebook.com (155 yd Masters tee / 145 yd member tee, retrieved 2026-08-13) and multiple other fetched pages.

**Bunker layout (ANCHORED, three independent sources agree):** One bunker in front (paired with a shaved bank feeding into Rae's Creek), two bunkers in back cut into an azalea-lined ledge. Confirmed independently by NBC Sports/Golf Channel's "Hole-by-hole look at Augusta National" (fetched directly, retrieved 2026-08-13: "3 total bunkers — 1 front bunker, 2 bunkers... replaced original single bunker behind green in 1951") and by The Fried Egg's hole-12 profile (via WebSearch synthesis, corroborating front bunker + shaved bank, back runoff + two bunkers on an azalea ledge). Golfguidebook.com's account differs slightly on bunker side ("front bunker on left side... back bunker on right side," implying only one back bunker) — a minor secondary disagreement noted but not treated as overturning the two-source convergence on "two back bunkers."

**Green shape and orientation (ANCHORED qualitatively):** Multiple independently fetched sources agree the green is a diagonal, shoe-sole-shaped surface, shallower on the right, deeper on the left, sloping back-to-front and toward the creek, with the Sunday (far-right) pin position sitting roughly 15 yards deeper into the green than a front-left pin. This qualitative geometry is consistent across every source fetched (PGA.com, The Fried Egg via search, golfguidebook.com).

**Green width/depth in yards (PUBLISHED but NOT reconcilable — flag, do not silently pick one):** Four fetched or search-synthesized sources give four different numeric pictures:
- SI.com (direct fetch): "approximately 60 feet at its greatest depth," resembling a shoe's sole.
- WebSearch synthesis of multiple pages (golfcompendium, liveabout, golfdigest, yourgolftravel, golfpass): green is "26 yards long from back to front," "only 20-feet deep" in places.
- WebSearch synthesis (thefriedegg, others): green is "16 yards wide," "33 yards deep."
- golfguidebook.com (direct fetch): green is "35 yards wide," with the middle section ("narrowest middle pin") only "9 yards deep," and the front-bunker landing area "about nine yards deep."

These numbers do not describe a single consistent rectangle by any reading — width claims run 16 to 35 yards (more than double), depth claims run 9 to 33 yards depending on which section of the green and which source. This is the same kind of hard disagreement 002 flagged for driving distance (Shot Scope vs. Arccos) rather than silently averaged. Augusta National does not publish official green dimensions; every number above is a journalist's or third-party site's estimate, and none of the four is more authoritative than the others on this specific point. The one number with the most corroboration and the most specific attribution (golfguidebook.com's "9 yards deep at the middle/narrowest pin") loosely matches the popular "10-12 paces" folklore cited in the release brief, but it is a single-source figure for one green section, not a validated overall dimension.

**Verdict: MODELED for precise width/depth** (use a stated range, e.g., front-third depth ~9-12 yd, overall green envelope roughly 16-35 yd wide by 20-33 yd deep at its longest diagonal, sourced and flagged as journalist-estimated, not architect-published). **ANCHORED for hazard layout, orientation, and yardage** (bunker count/position, diagonal shoe-sole shape, Sunday-pin depth offset, 155-yard tee distance).

## Anchor 4 — Masters hole-12 scoring history and the 2019 final round

**Status: Scoring history PUBLISHED and cross-consistent. 2019 final-round narrative PUBLISHED, cross-consistent after resolving one real discrepancy caught in this hunt (see flag below).**

**All-time scoring average (ANCHORED, two independent direct fetches converge):** SI.com (direct fetch, retrieved 2026-08-13): all-time scoring average 3.27 (stated elsewhere on the same page as 3.267), lowest year 3.03 (2002), highest year 3.55 (1966). NBC Sports/Golf Channel "Hole-by-hole look at Augusta National" (direct fetch, retrieved 2026-08-13): scoring average 3.28. A WebSearch synthesis pulling from a different set of pages (golfcompendium, liveabout, pressreader) independently states the same low (3.03, 2002) and high (3.556, 1966) years. Three convergent readings on the same two extreme years is a strong cross-check even though the sources are all secondary aggregators, not masters.com itself (masters.com's own historical-stats pages were not found rendering usable numbers in this hunt). **Note:** the NBC Sports fetch's difficulty-rank label ("4th easiest") contradicts SI.com's own difficulty-rank label ("4th hardest, all-time difficulty rank 4") for what looks like the same underlying number — flagging this specific rank label as unreliable/likely a fetch-summarization error, while the scoring-average values themselves (3.27-3.28) are corroborated and kept.

**2019 season shot distribution (ANCHORED, independently confirmed twice):** Racing Post's Augusta National hole-by-hole guide (direct fetch, retrieved 2026-08-13) gives, for 2019: eagles 0, birdies 52, pars 200, bogeys 38, double-bogeys-or-worse 14, with a stated 2019 scoring average of 3.053. This exact 52/200/38/14 breakdown also surfaced independently in a WebSearch synthesis of a separate page before the Racing Post fetch was run, which is a genuine cross-check, not a duplicate read of the same URL.

**2019 final-round water-ball list (ANCHORED after resolving a source conflict found in this hunt):** Golf.com's day-of 2019 article, PGATOUR.com's 2020 "Remembering the final round" retrospective, and a separate WebSearch synthesis of Sky Sports/other independent coverage all confirm the same four players found Rae's Creek off the tee at 12 in the final round, in the same short window: **Brooks Koepka** (9-iron, drifted right, bank, rolled back into the water), **Ian Poulter** (8-iron, didn't carry, water), **Francesco Molinari** (tee shot into the bank, rolled back into the creek), **Tony Finau** (also found the water). All four made double bogey. Wind is described (PGATOUR.com retrospective, direct fetch) as gusting to "around 20 mph" at the time.

**Discrepancy caught and resolved:** the PGATOUR.com retrospective's account of Molinari includes a detail — "chipped from the left intermediate rough... clipped a tree branch and fell into the water" — that does NOT belong to hole 12. A follow-up search (golf.com, Sky Sports "Molinari puts two in water during final round") confirms this chip-off-a-tree-into-the-water shot happened on the par-5 15th hole later in the round, a second and separate water ball for Molinari that day. The first WebFetch pass on the PGATOUR.com page merged the 12th-hole and 15th-hole content into one bullet; this log corrects that before it could enter the 003 codebase as a factual error about hole 12 specifically. This is exactly the kind of error this gate exists to catch, and is why the 2019 list above cites Molinari's tee shot (hole 12), not the tree-deflected chip (hole 15).

**Verdict: ANCHORED** for both the all-time/2019 scoring statistics and the 2019 final-round water-ball narrative, each corroborated by at least two independently fetched sources, with one real cross-source error caught and corrected during this hunt rather than carried through silently.

## Anchor 6 — Wind at hole 12 (narrative context)

**Status: Journalism-only, as expected and labeled per the task's own framing — not a data anchor, a narrative one.**

Multiple golf-journalism sources, none claiming to quantify wind statistically, converge on the same qualitative picture: the tee sits in the lee of the 11th green and surrounding pines while the green itself is exposed to wind funneling along the creek bed and swirling inside the Amen Corner amphitheater, so — per the line attributed variously to Jack Nicklaus, Ben Crenshaw, and Tom Kite and repeated across several fetched/searched pages — "the wind on the tee is almost never the wind at the green." SI.com (direct fetch) quantifies the practical effect as "up to a four-club differential based on direction and speed." PGATOUR.com's retrospective (direct fetch) puts the 2019 final-round gust at "around 20 mph." Herbert Warren Wind's 1958 Golf Digest coining of "Amen Corner" itself (referenced via WebSearch, not directly fetched at length) is the origin of the stretch's name and is safe to cite as historical/journalistic color.

**Verdict: NARRATIVE ANCHOR, labeled as such.** No numeric wind-rose or club-selection-shift data was found or expected; this is context for prose, not an input to the oval-inversion math.

## Anchor 7 — Tour approach proximity, 150-160 yards

**Status: PUBLISHED at the target yardage band, sourced from PGA Tour's own "Approaches from 150-175 yards" statistic (fairway and rough tracked separately) as quoted by three independent third-party sites. Direct fetch of pgatour.com's stat pages failed (see note below); the anchor rests on the same style of third-party-blog-quoting-pgatour.com sourcing this log already used for Anchor 1's Arccos/Shot Scope figures.**

**Fetch note:** pgatour.com/stats/detail/338 and the adjacent stat IDs (339, 02367) are client-rendered; every direct WebFetch attempt against them returned a live tournament leaderboard shell instead of the stat table, regardless of prompt. This is a tool limitation, not a missing-data finding — the underlying stat pages exist and are cited correctly by name and ID below, but this hunt could not fetch their raw HTML directly. Three independent sites that did successfully extract the numbers stand in for a direct fetch.

**Mean proximity, 150-175 yd, fairway (ANCHORED, three independent sources, small season-to-season spread):**
- Mike Bury Golf, ["PGA Tour Averages: Proximity To Hole 50-200 Yards"](https://mikebury.com/2023/10/22/pga-tour-averages-proximity-to-hole-50-200-yards/). Retrieved 2026-08-16. States a full table sourced to PGA Tour data through the 2022-23 season (through the John Deere Classic): 150-175 yd, 28'9"; 125-150 yd, 23'8"; 175-200 yd, 35'9".
- Golf Insider UK, ["How Good Do Your Approach Shots Need To Be?"](https://golfinsideruk.com/approach-play-stats/), citing pgatour.com. Retrieved 2026-08-16. States 150-175 yd: 64% GIR, average proximity "a shade under 30ft"; 125-150 yd: 69.3% GIR, average proximity 23 ft.
- The Left Rough, ["The Truth about Greens in Regulation"](https://theleftrough.com/why-greens-in-regulation-matter/), citing [pgatour.com/stats/stat.103.html](https://www.pgatour.com/stats/stat.103.html) directly by name. Retrieved 2026-08-16. States 150-175 yd: 63% of greens hit, average proximity 27 feet; 125-150 yd: 69% of greens hit, average proximity 23 feet.
- Older cross-check, The GOLFTEC Scramble, ["Golf Stats: PGA Tour player approach shots"](https://scramble.golftec.com/blog/2016/10/golf-stats-pga-tour-approach-shots/). Retrieved 2026-08-16. 2016-season figures: 150-175 yd, 63.95% GIR, 27'6" from the fairway, 41'5" from the rough; 100-125 yd, 74.59% GIR, 20'0" fairway / 30'3" rough.

Three sources spanning three different data-pull dates (2016, 2022-23, and an undated recent pull behind Golf Insider UK's "shade under 30ft") converge tightly: mean proximity 27 to 29 feet from the fairway at 150-175 yards, GIR 63 to 64%. The adjacent 125-150 yd band (23 to 24 ft, 69% GIR) brackets the 155-yard shot from the other side, letting the 150-175 figure stand without needing interpolation, since 155 falls inside that bucket's own range rather than between two buckets.

**From-rough proximity (ANCHORED, one clear figure, one leaderboard-only figure):** GOLFTEC's 2016 figure gives a tour-average rough proximity of 41'5" at 150-175 yd, well worse than the 27'6" fairway figure from the same source and season, a direct from-fairway-vs-rough split at the target distance. PGA Tour's own rough-lie stat page (["Approaches from 150-175 yards (Rgh)"](https://www.pgatour.com/stats/detail/02367)) could not be fetched directly for its tour average (same client-rendering issue above); a WebSearch snippet surfaced only the individual leader's figure (Viktor Hovland, 33'8"), which is a best-player number, not a tour average, and is not used as the anchor here.

**Distribution-shape signal (WEAKER SOURCING, single citation, not independently corroborated):** A GolfWRX article, ["Pros v. Amateurs: Shots from 100 yards and 150 yards"](https://golfwrx.com/271279/pros-v-amateurs-shots-from-100-yards-and-150-yards/), reportedly states that PGA Tour pros from 160 yards finish inside 3 feet only about 1% of the time and inside 6 feet only about 5% of the time. Direct fetch of this page returned HTTP 403 both times it was attempted; the figures above come from a WebSearch result snippet quoting the page, not from a verified full-text read. If accurate, this describes a strongly right-skewed proximity distribution at this yardage (a 27-29 ft mean with only 1% of shots inside 3 ft is not a simple Gaussian-radial miss pattern), which matters for shaping the oval's inner region, but this specific figure should be re-verified by direct fetch or an alternate mirror before it drives a specific sd number, the same caveat this log already applied to Anchor 5's sand-save figures.

**Verdict: ANCHORED** for mean proximity (27-29 ft, sensitivity range) and GIR% (63-64%) from the fairway at 150-175 yards, the bucket that contains the 12th hole's 155-yard shot, corroborated by three independently-sourced quotes of PGA Tour's own stat page across three different data-pull windows. **ANCHORED** for the qualitative from-fairway-vs-rough gap (rough proximity meaningfully worse, ~41 ft vs ~27-28 ft in the one source with both figures from the same season). **MODELED** for turning that mean-and-GIR% pair into the Tour tier's 2D oval standard deviations — no source in this hunt (or in Anchor 2) publishes a numeric tour-level dispersion sd or dist/dir ratio for the 150-160 yard band directly; the conversion must follow the same three-step inference chain already disclosed in the GATE VERDICT below (radial-scale inversion via GIR%-through-green-geometry, then the Anchor 2 anisotropy split), using this anchor's mean/GIR% as the tier's radial-scale input in place of Anchor 1's amateur figures. The 1%-inside-3-ft / 5%-inside-6-ft shape figure is a secondary, weakly-sourced sanity check on that inversion, not a load-bearing number.

## GATE VERDICT

**(a) Invertibility gate: PASS**, with three named, disclosed inference steps.

1. Convert Anchor 1's published proximity/GIR-distance figures (Arccos "magic number" GIR-50% distance per tier, Arccos/Shot Scope foot-proximity at 125-149 yd for at least the 10-handicap tier) into a radial (combined distance+line) miss-distance scale per tier, by the same style of probability-inversion 002 used to turn Shot Scope's fairway-hit % into a lateral standard deviation through a known fairway width. Directly supported for scratch/5/10-handicap; interpolated/extrapolated for 15 through 30 using the shape of the published GIR-distance curve.
2. Split that radial scale into separate distance-error and line-error standard deviations using Broadie's ~3:1 amateur distance:direction anisotropy ratio (Anchor 2), extended from its published short-game/sand distance band to the 100-160-yard approach band. This step is explicitly MODELED, not measured, and should ship with the 2:1-3.5:1 sensitivity range recorded in Anchor 2.
3. Place the resulting two-axis oval against Augusta 12's hazard geometry (Anchor 3) — bunker positions, creek edge, green orientation are ANCHORED and safe to use directly; the exact green width/depth numbers are not, and should be represented as a range rather than a single figure pulled from any one of the four disagreeing sources.

Every one of these three steps has a named, disclosed anchor behind it, and every place a step is an assumption rather than a measurement is labeled MODELED with a stated sensitivity range, matching the standard 002 set for this gate.

**(b) Masters hole-12 scoring + geometry, citable at publication quality: PARTIALLY — the scoring history and the 2019 final-round narrative both clear the bar; the green's precise numeric dimensions do not.** The all-time scoring average, the 2019 shot-distribution breakdown, and the 2019 water-ball sequence (players, clubs, wind) are each corroborated by at least two independently fetched sources, and one real cross-source factual error (the Molinari hole-12-vs-hole-15 mixup) was caught and corrected in this hunt rather than shipped. That clears publication quality on its own terms. Green width/depth, by contrast, has four fetched sources disagreeing by more than 2x on both dimensions with no arbitration possible from anything found this session — that specific claim does not clear publication quality as a precise figure and must run as a sourced range or be dropped to qualitative description ("shallow, diagonal, shoe-sole-shaped") rather than a quoted number. Per the locked decision, this does not on its own force the 2019 chapter to narrative-only, since the 2019 scoring/water-ball material that chapter actually depends on is independently well-sourced; it does mean any chart or caption that states a specific green-yardage number needs a visible range and citation rather than a single confident figure.

**Weakest anchor found, and its MODELED sensitivity range:** the distance-vs-line anisotropy ratio for 100-160-yard approach irons (Anchor 2). No source anywhere in this hunt publishes a numeric dist/dir ratio for that specific distance band — the only numeric ratio found (Broadie, ~3:1) is measured on 20-60-yard short game and 0-50-yard sand shots, and the 100-150-yard ellipses in the same paper are described only qualitatively. Carry it as MODELED with a **2:1 to 3.5:1 sensitivity range**, floored below the published short-game ratio (approach irons likely somewhat less distance-dominated than chips/sand shots) and capped at the published amateur short-game value (3.1-3.3:1) as the upper bound.

**Follow-up, 2026-08-16:** The open anchor named in the spec (published Tour approach proximity at 150-160 yards, needed for the Tour oval) is now resolved. Anchor 7 puts a mean proximity of 27-29 ft and a GIR% of 63-64% ANCHORED at the 150-175 yd band, corroborated by three independently-sourced quotes of PGA Tour's own stat page across three different seasons, with the from-fairway-vs-rough split also ANCHORED. What remains MODELED is unchanged in kind from the rest of this log: converting that mean/GIR% pair into the Tour tier's two-axis oval standard deviations still runs through the same disclosed three-step chain (radial-scale inversion, then Anchor 2's anisotropy split), and Anchor 2's anisotropy ratio remains this release's single weakest link, now applying to five tiers (scratch, 5, 10, 15/20 interpolated, and Tour) instead of four. The validation gate in ticket #9 (Tour oval reproducing hole 12's 3.27-3.28 scoring average and the 2019 outcome distribution) can now be built on an ANCHORED proximity input rather than a placeholder.
