# Golf data sources for analysis 003 (scoping survey)

Retrieved 2026-08-13. Every claim below links to the primary source I pulled it from. Where I could not verify a claim against a primary source (a paywalled dashboard, a Kaggle page that would not render), I say so instead of guessing.

## 1. DataGolf API

**What's in it.** The [API Access page](https://datagolf.com/api-access) lists endpoints across six groups: general use (player IDs, tour schedules, field updates), model predictions (DG Rankings, pre-tournament win/finish probabilities, skill decompositions, fantasy projections), a live model (predictions and stats updating every 5 minutes during a tournament, hole-scoring distributions), betting tools (outright and matchup odds across multiple sportsbooks), and historical data (round-level scoring and strokes gained across 22 tours, event finishes, historical odds, historical DFS salaries).

**Granularity.** This needs a correction to what the marketing page implies. DataGolf's own [FAQ](https://datagolf.com/frequently-asked-questions) states the company has "a partnership with the PGA Tour that gives us access to shot-level data from their ShotLink program" but that "this data is not publicly available." The same FAQ says DataGolf's baseline predictive model uses only round-level inputs, no hole- or shot-level data. Read together: DataGolf ingests ShotLink shot-level data internally to build its strokes-gained model, then exposes round-level and hole-level aggregates through the public API. An outside analyst cannot pull individual shot coordinates or club selection through this API. The [Raw Data Notes](https://datagolf.com/raw-data-notes) page confirms round-level scoring and strokes-gained detail for PGA Tour (full history), DP World Tour and Korn Ferry (Oct 2020 forward, manually QC'd), LIV Golf (round-level SG since 2024), and several smaller tours, with a documented changelog of corrections to specific events.

**Pricing.** Confirmed from the [Subscribe page](https://datagolf.com/subscribe): Scratch Basic is $20/month or $190/year, no API access. Scratch Plus is $30/month or $270/year and includes the API, but the monthly Plus plan excludes the historical archive endpoints — the annual plan is required for archive access.

**Republication terms.** The [Terms and Conditions](https://datagolf.com/terms-and-conditions) grant "limited, non-exclusive, revocable permission to make personal, non-commercial use of the content" and state plainly: "you will not redistribute or transfer any of the Data Golf Services or Contents." No carve-out exists for attributed charts, derived analysis, or editorial use. A public golf-analytics site publishing charts built on DataGolf numbers is, on a literal reading, outside what the consumer ToS permits. I could not find a published commercial/media licensing page or price sheet distinct from the consumer subscription; any legitimate use for a public article would need a direct conversation with DataGolf, not just a Scratch Plus signup.

**Verdict.** Best modeling and prediction data available to an independent analyst, but the licensing terms block public republication as written, and the shot-level granularity needed for a dispersion or ball-flight animation simply is not in the API. Usable for round/event-level stories (win probability over a tournament, strokes-gained trends) only after resolving the redistribution restriction directly with DataGolf.

## 2. PGA Tour ShotLink

**Who can access it, and how.** ShotLink is the Tour's volunteer-operated, laser/camera shot-capture system, run with CDW and (per [an AWS case study](https://aws.amazon.com/sports/pgatour/shotlink-pro/)) rebuilt as "ShotLink Pro" on AWS infrastructure. The [PGA Tour's own ShotLink page](https://www.pgatour.com/shotlink) frames it as a partner-and-broadcast data pipeline, not a public data product.

**Media/research program.** There was a formal channel: a [ShotLink Intelligence Data request form](https://registrations.pgatourhq.com/forms/shotlinkintel/). As of this retrieval, that page states the program "is being re-evaluated and access to ShotLink data is not currently available," with the Tour saying it is "exploring alternatives." This is a dead end right now, not a live path.

**Media accreditation route.** The [Media Regulations PDF](https://pgatourmedia.pgatourhq.com/static-assets/page/header/files/media-regulations-2020-september.pdf) and related credentialing pages describe eligibility for press credentials (established outlets, or digital outlets publishing 60%+ original editorial content at least three times a week) — freelancers without an assigning outlet do not qualify. Even for accredited media, the regulations state that "scoring data, statistical information and other data provided by PGA TOUR to a media outlet are provided solely to enhance the media outlet's news and editorial coverage" and may not be redistributed "unless a separate data license agreement is entered into with PGA TOUR."

**Verdict.** Not realistically available to an independent creator right now: the intelligence-data application program is paused, and the media-credential path both gates eligibility on being an established outlet and prohibits redistributing any data feed without a separate license. Dead end for 003 unless the owner wants to email PGA Tour media relations directly and wait on a business-development response.

## 3. USGA / R&A Distance Insights

**What's public.** The USGA and R&A jointly publish an [Annual Driving Distance Report](https://www.globalgolfpost.com/featured/usga-ra-publish-conclusions-of-distance-insights-project/) drawing on driving-distance data from seven men's and women's professional tours worldwide, plus the original 2020 [Distance Insights Report](https://www.randa.org/en/articles/distance-insights-report-released) documenting a 100-year trend of increasing hitting distance and course length. The hub page is [usga.org/distanceinsights](https://www.usga.org/content/usga/home-page/advancing-the-game/distance-insights.html); I could not verify a downloadable per-player or per-tour raw dataset behind it — the page returned an HTTP 403 to automated fetch during this research, so I saw it only through search snippets, not the primary source directly. Treat "downloadable dataset" as unconfirmed until someone loads that page in a browser.

**Regulatory context, not raw data.** Recent activity here is rulemaking, not data release: a [June 17, 2026 notice](https://www.globalgolfpost.com/featured/usga-ra-publish-conclusions-of-distance-insights-project/) to manufacturers on Overall Distance Standard (ODS) testing changes, with no change to testing until January 2030.

**Verdict.** Good for a paragraph of authoritative context ("the governing bodies say distance has risen for a century and are still deciding what to do about it") and for tour-level aggregate driving-distance trend lines. Not a shot-level or animatable dataset. Confirm the 403 issue and check for a literal downloadable file before scoping any deliverable around it.

## 4. Consumer shot-tracking aggregate publications

**Arccos.** Publishes an [annual Driving Distance Report](https://www.arccosgolf.com/blogs/community/arccos-releases-golfer-driving-distance-annual-report-featuring-the-most-shots-ever-analyzed). The [2026 edition](https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/6-insights-from-the-2026-arccos-driving-distance-report/) is built on more than 5 million rounds and nearly 10 million driver tee shots from 2025, an eight-year trend line by age, gender, and handicap, drawn from a total dataset of 1.5+ billion shots across 25 million rounds in 160+ countries. This is press-release/blog content, not a raw data download — you get charts and summary stats, not rows.

**Shot Scope.** Publishes an [Annual Golf Performance Report](https://golfbusinessnews.com/news/practice-range-and-teaching/shot-scope-publishes-2025-golf-performance-report/), the 2026 edition analyzing 870,000+ rounds and ~74 million shots from amateurs in 124 countries, broken into scoring, tee shots, approach, short game, and putting benchmarks. Sold as an [ebook](https://shotscope.com/us/shop/products/ebooks/shot-scope-data-report-26/), so even the "free" summary version is marketing content, and the full report may be a paid product. Shot Scope has also published a specialized "Women's Game, By the Numbers" report covering 2.5 million shots.

**Golfmetrics.** An app built by Mark Broadie, the academic who invented strokes gained, per [search-indexed material from golfmetrics.com](https://golfmetrics.com/faq/). Its database includes 100,000+ amateur shots for benchmarking by skill level, with strokes-gained breakdowns by category (drive, approach at 100-150/150-200/200-250/250+, short game, putting). No evidence of a public raw-data export; this is a personal-tracking app with in-app aggregate benchmarks, not a publishable dataset.

**Verdict.** All three are citable as secondary sources for aggregate claims (e.g., "amateur driving distance rose X yards since 2018, per Arccos"), each with its own methodology and sample. None gives raw, row-level data you can re-analyze or animate from scratch. Good for context sentences and comparison points, not for the visualization's data backbone.

## 5. Free / scrapeable sources

**PGA Tour stats site.** [pgatour.com/stats](https://www.pgatour.com/stats) has no public developer API — confirmed no self-serve portal exists. I checked [pgatour.com/robots.txt](https://www.pgatour.com) directly: it disallows a handful of query-parameter patterns and a few specific paths (`/bin/data/`, `/elements/`, `/media-guide/`) but does not blanket-disallow `/stats`, and it publishes a `stats_sitemap.xml`. That means casual scraping of published stats pages is not blocked at the robots.txt level, though the site's Media Regulations (see ShotLink section above) still restrict redistributing any data obtained through an official media relationship. Unofficial wrappers exist, e.g. a documented [Parse.bot PGA Tour API](https://parse.bot/marketplace/cf113789-5b7d-41d0-8578-efb22dc9a9e0/pgatour-com-api) and community scraper scripts on GitHub (`zachwill/golf`, `btatkinson/golf_scraper`) — third-party, unofficial, and could break at any time.

**ESPN.** ESPN runs a well-documented [unofficial/undocumented API](https://github.com/pseudo-r/Public-ESPN-API) (also catalogued in [this gist](https://gist.github.com/akeaswaran/b48b02f1c94f873c6655e7129910fc3b)) at `site.web.api.espn.com`, no key or signup required. The golf leaderboard/player-summary endpoint returns per-round, hole-by-hole linescores (value, par, score type) plus stats — genuinely useful, hole-level granularity, free. No published rate limit or SLA since it's not an official product; cache aggressively and don't hammer it.

**OWGR.** No public API from owgr.com itself. Third-party access exists via [Sportradar's Golf API](https://developer.sportradar.com/golf/reference/golf-official-world-golf-rankings) (opaque, custom-negotiated pricing, reportedly starting in the low five figures per month per [one industry cost guide](https://www.lsports.eu/blog/sports-data-cost/) — well outside an independent creator's budget) or via DataGolf's rankings endpoint, which folds in OWGR rank per player. A community [OWGR scraper](https://github.com/bradklassen/Official_World_Golf_Ranking_Scraper) and a matching [Kaggle mirror](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/bradklassen/official-world-golf-ranking-data) exist for historical ranking-points data.

**Kaggle.** Multiple PGA Tour datasets exist: [robikscube/pga-tour-golf-data-20152022](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/robikscube/pga-tour-golf-data-20152022) and [jmpark746/pga-tour-data-2010-2018](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/jmpark746/pga-tour-data-2010-2018), plus notebooks analyzing ShotLink-derived course rankings. I was not able to confirm exact license terms or column-level schema for these through automated fetch (Kaggle's dataset pages are JS-rendered and returned only page titles to WebFetch) — check the license badge on the dataset page directly before relying on one for a public piece, since Kaggle licenses are set per-uploader and vary (CC0, CC-BY, etc.).

**"ProGolfWiki."** I found no such resource. Searches turned up generic golf-course-database products (iGolf, Maps4Golf, Golfbert, AllGolfHoles) but nothing matching that name. Treat this as a dead end unless the owner has a different spelling or source in mind.

**Weather APIs.** Several usable free tiers for joining weather to tournament rounds by date/location: [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/) (free, no key, historical back to 1940), [Visual Crossing](https://www.visualcrossing.com/weather-api/) (1,000 free records/day, 50 years of hourly history), [WeatherAPI.com](https://www.weatherapi.com/) (free tier, hourly/15-min/historical, has a sports-data angle), [Xweather](https://www.xweather.com/products/weather-api) (15,000 free monthly calls). Open-Meteo is the strongest fit for a one-off analysis: no signup friction, no rate-limit risk at this project's scale.

**Verdict.** This tier is where the real, freely republishable, engineering-friendly data lives: ESPN's hidden API for hole-by-hole scoring, Open-Meteo for weather joins, Kaggle for a bulk historical base table (once license-checked). None of it is shot-level, so it supports round/hole-level animations (leaderboard movement, scoring-average-by-hole, weather-vs-scoring) rather than ball-flight or dispersion visuals.

## 6. Shot-level / trajectory-level data for an animated visualization

This is the weak point across every source above. Direct findings:

- **DataGolf**: shot-level ShotLink data feeds their model internally but is explicitly not exposed via the public API (see Section 1).
- **PGA Tour ShotLink direct access**: paused/unavailable (see Section 2).
- **TrackMan/Toptracer**: both use proprietary radar/camera systems ([TrackMan parameter docs](https://support.trackmangolf.com/hc/en-us/articles/5089892383515-Practice-Trackman-Data-Parameter-Definitions), [Toptracer overview](https://toptracer.com/tv/)) with no public data export. One individual published a ~10,000-row personal TrackMan capture on GitHub ([tim-blackmore/launch-monitor-regression](https://github.com/tim-blackmore/launch-monitor-regression)) — a personal dataset, not a citable public source, and far too small to visualize meaningfully at tour scale.
- **Win probability over a tournament**: this is the one genuinely animatable, shot-free option. DataGolf's live model produces finish probabilities updating every 5 minutes during a live tournament and publishes a [live win-probability page](https://datagolf.com/live-stats/pga-tour) on their own site — but per Section 1's licensing read, pulling this via API for a public animated chart on Dogleg Data would need the redistribution question resolved with DataGolf first. Built independently from ESPN's free hole-by-hole scoring feed plus a homemade win-probability model, this becomes buildable without any licensing conflict — more engineering work, but clean.
- **Strokes-gained flow / dispersion visuals**: consumer apps like [Shot Pattern](https://shotpattern.app/) build shot-dispersion and strokes-gained visuals for individual golfers' own rounds, not tour-wide public data. No public tour-level dispersion dataset surfaced in this research.

**Verdict.** Nothing surfaced gives free, licensable, shot-level or trajectory-level tour data. The one credible animated-viz path that doesn't fight a licensing restriction is a self-built round-by-round or hole-by-hole win-probability/strokes-gained-proxy model, fed by ESPN's free hole-level scoring API and DataGolf's round-level historical archive (if the licensing conversation with DataGolf resolves favorably) or by ESPN data alone (fully clean, but a rougher model without official strokes-gained inputs).
