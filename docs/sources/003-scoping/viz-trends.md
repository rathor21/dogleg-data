# Animated data-viz trends for analysis 003 (static site + LinkedIn)

Retrieved 2026-08-13. Research question: what animated data-viz formats are working in sports data journalism and on LinkedIn right now, and how would a solo golf-analytics creator ship one piece that works both embedded on a static site and posted natively to LinkedIn.

## 1. Formats

### Scrollytelling
The New York Times built [342,000 Swings Later, Derek Jeter Calls It a Career](https://novosmedios.gal/dsi/2015/06/07/342000-swings-later-derek-jeter-calls-it-a-career-the-new-york-times/) (Shan Carter, Joe Ward, David Waldstein, Mike Bostock, 2014). A single field of glyphs represents every swing of Jeter's career; the reader starts zoomed into one glyph and scrolling semantically zooms out until the full career is visible. The trick that made it spread: the chart *is* the story, text is minimal, and the scroll position drives real computation (aggregation level), not just a fade-in.

The Pudding wrote up their own scrollytelling stack and compared six implementation libraries: [How to implement scrollytelling with six different libraries](https://pudding.cool/process/how-to-implement-scrollytelling/). Their take: Waypoints and ScrollMagic suit fully custom stories, graph-scroll.js suits D3-first projects, and rolling your own gives full control at the cost of build time. This is the most direct "how do I actually build this" source available.

### Bar-chart races
John Burn-Murdoch's Financial Times animation of [the world's most populous cities over 500 years](https://observablehq.com/@johnburnmurdoch/bar-chart-race-the-most-populous-cities-in-the-world) (2019) is the piece that put a name on the format and made it a Twitter staple. Mike Bostock republished a reproducible version as an Observable notebook, which is why the technique diffused so fast, other creators could fork the code directly. What made it spread: a familiar visual verb (racing/overtaking) applied to a topic with obvious stakes (which city is #1), no reading required to get the point in the first three seconds.

### Animated small multiples / relative shot charts
Owen Phillips runs [The F5](https://thef5.substack.com/), a solo-operator NBA analytics newsletter, exactly the model Dogleg Data is aiming for. His piece [Hot Shots](https://thef5.substack.com/p/hot-shots) maps each player's shot frequency against league average (red = shoots more than average from that zone, gray = less) and drops in short animated GIFs of individual plays as annotations (e.g., a Trae Young three with the caption "18 seconds on the shot clock, btw"). Actual engagement on that piece: 44 shares, 2 reposts, 4 comments, useful as a realistic baseline rather than a viral outlier. It's a good model for effort level: static R-built charts plus a handful of short GIF inserts, not full animation.

### Particle/flow visualizations
This format is real in data journalism generally (network flows, migration, weather) but I could not find a primary-source sports example that's actually "particle/flow" rather than a line chart or bar race mislabeled as one. FlowingData's [sports tag](https://flowingdata.com/tag/sports/) and [Washington Post tag](https://flowingdata.com/tag/washington-post/) are the closest survey sources. Treat this as a low-priority format for 003, it's higher production cost (usually Canvas/WebGL particle systems) for a payoff that isn't clearly proven in sports specifically.

### Animated shot/dispersion maps
Golf-specific and directly relevant. [golf-flight.com](https://itwasdns.io/2025/02/17/visualizing-golf-shot-trajectories-with-react-and-three-js/) is a solo developer's project built with React, React Three Fiber, and Three.js: enter swing parameters (face angle, path, speed, launch angle) and watch a physics-simulated ball flight with a tracer path render in 3D. The developer's own note is useful for effort-scoping: "it took me more time than I care to admit to get this canvas look somewhat presentable," and the project still has known mobile layout issues. This is the higher-effort end of the spectrum. On the lower-effort end, [Shot Pattern](https://shotpattern.app/) overlays a static "shot cone" dispersion ellipse on a hole map without any 3D engine.

### Win-probability rivers
FiveThirtyEight's [live in-game win probability](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/our-new-live-in-game-win-probabilities-are-here-for-the-nba-playoffs/) model (NBA playoffs) is the reference implementation: a probability line that updates play-by-play. Note this property is now archived (538 moved to ABC News), so it's a historical reference rather than something to copy pixel-for-pixel, but the format itself (a river/band chart that narrates a game's swings) is well proven. No obvious golf equivalent exists yet (win probability during a single round is a much noisier signal than in a possession-based sport), which is a gap 003 could fill.

### Video explainers with motion graphics
Not covered in depth here, no primary sports example turned up that clearly outperformed the formats above, and it's the highest production cost of the group (voiceover, edit, motion graphics software). Deprioritized for a solo builder.

## 2. Sports-specific exemplars

**F1 telemetry.** [FastF1](https://github.com/theOehrly/Fast-F1) is the real, verifiable tool underlying most independent F1 telemetry accounts: an MIT-licensed Python package for F1 timing/telemetry data, 5.3k GitHub stars as of this research. One SEO blog ([BrightCoding](https://www.blog.brightcoding.dev/2025/10/30/%F0%9F%8F%8E%EF%B8%8F-the-ultimate-fast-f1-guide-unlock-formula-1-data-like-a-pro-2025-edition)) claims a "19-year-old Singapore student" predicted a Perez pit stop live on Twitch, hit 3.2M views in 48 hours, and drove FastF1's stars from 1.9k to 7.4k overnight. I could not corroborate this anywhere else, no Twitch clip, no news coverage, and it contradicts the repo's actual current star count (5.3k, not 7.4k+). Treat that specific anecdote as unverified marketing copy, not a citable fact. The tool itself is real and the pattern (telemetry comparison charts built on FastF1 output) is a legitimate exemplar category, just don't repeat the viral-growth number.

**Soccer/xG.** [Understat](https://understat.com/) publishes public team/player xG charts that get reshared after big matches. [The Analyst (Opta)](https://theanalyst.com/articles/interactive-snapshot-player-xg-proportions) publishes an interactive player xG-proportions snapshot as part of its regular content. The xGPhilosophy account on X (half a million followers per search results, not independently verified here) is the community reference point for xG shot-map memes and comparisons.

**NBA shot charts.** Owen Phillips's [The F5](https://thef5.substack.com/) (see above) is the best documented solo-creator exemplar with real, modest engagement numbers rather than inflated ones.

**Golf-specific.** [DataGolf](https://datagolf.com/data-viz-of-the-week) runs a "Data Viz of the Week" series, a new interactive/static chart on a golf topic weekly, shared to their [X account](https://x.com/DataGolf). Lou Stagner built a following as a "golf nut and data nerd" combining a BI background with Arccos's shot-level dataset ([Arccos hire announcement](https://www.arccosgolf.com/blogs/community/arccos-golf-announces-hiring-of-renowned-golf-data-analyst-lou-stagner)); his output is mostly static charts posted natively to X/LinkedIn rather than heavy animation, worth noting because it suggests the golf data-content audience currently rewards clear, well-labeled static charts as much as motion.

## 3. LinkedIn mechanics 2025-2026

Caveat up front: none of these are LinkedIn's own published numbers, LinkedIn does not release format-level reach data. Every source below is a third-party marketing blog inferring patterns from client accounts or aggregated tools, so treat the specific multipliers as directional, not precise.

- **Native video vs. carousel, the picture flipped in 2025.** Earlier in the algorithm cycle, native video reportedly out-reached other formats (captions cited as adding up to 112% reach, [Closely](https://blog.closelyhq.com/linkedin-video-vs-image-vs-text-algorithm-preferences-revealed/)). By late 2025/2026, document carousels overtook video: carousels reportedly get 2-3x the average organic reach of other formats, with document carousels averaging ~1,387 impressions vs. ~703 for image posts and ~589 for text-only ([Closely](https://blog.closelyhq.com/linkedin-video-vs-image-vs-text-algorithm-preferences-revealed/); consistent with [ContentIn's](https://contentin.io/blog/linkedin-algorithm-2025-the-complete-content-format-strategy-guide/) framing that video "went from king to commoner" in 2025 while carousels became "the biggest reach multiplier of any format"). ContentIn also notes ideal carousel length dropped from ~12-13 slides to 6-9 due to "carousel fatigue," and that carousel click-through below 35% triggers a visibility penalty.
- **Why carousels win on the algorithm's terms.** The mechanism cited across sources is dwell time: swiping through a carousel keeps a viewer on the post for 20-60+ seconds, which the algorithm reads as a strong quality signal ([Closely](https://blog.closelyhq.com/linkedin-video-vs-image-vs-text-algorithm-preferences-revealed/)).
- **Video specs if you do post video.** Best engagement clusters between 30 seconds and 3 minutes, with videos under 60 seconds in 4:5 vertical format getting the most comments. Feed video should be square (1:1, 1080x1080) or vertical (4:5, 1080x1350), not widescreen, MP4/H.264 is the standard container ([InVideo LinkedIn video specs](https://invideo.io/blog/linkedin-video-size-specification/)).
- **Silent autoplay is the default viewing condition.** Roughly 80-85% of LinkedIn video is watched muted ([one widely cited stat via a LinkedIn native post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/digi-media_85-of-viewerswatch-videos-with-the-sound-activity-7171458816843218945-HUqV); repeated across secondary sources). Captions/on-screen text reportedly add 21-29% engagement and reduce scroll-past rate roughly 2.7x ([summarized across sources, no single primary LinkedIn citation found](https://www.opus.pro/blog/linkedin-video-caption-subtitle-best-practices)). Practical implication: any video for 003 needs to read correctly with the sound off, treat captions/on-chart labels as mandatory, not optional.

**Net for 003:** a document-style carousel (a sequence of static, well-labeled chart frames, essentially "stills" pulled from the animated piece) is currently the highest-reach LinkedIn-native format, ahead of native video. That argues for producing the site animation in a way that also yields clean static keyframes for a carousel cut, rather than treating video-of-the-animation as the only social deliverable.

## 4. Technical delivery for a static site

Rough effort levels assume a solo builder working in an existing static-site pipeline (per `.impeccable.md` design system).

| Approach | What it's good for | Effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scroll-driven CSS (`animation-timeline: scroll()` / `view()`)** | Simple reveal/progress animations tied to scroll, no JS dependency | Low | Chrome/Edge 115+ and Safari 18+ (landed Safari 26 beta per [WebKit's guide](https://webkit.org/blog/17101/a-guide-to-scroll-driven-animations-with-just-css/)) support it; Firefox is still behind a flag as of mid-2026 ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_properties_animation-timeline_scroll)). Fine as a progressive enhancement, not safe as the only mechanism if Firefox readers matter. |
| **Scrollama.js + D3** | Chart state changes triggered at scroll checkpoints (the Pudding/NYT scrollytelling pattern) | Medium | [scrollama](https://github.com/russellsamora/scrollama) uses IntersectionObserver, is lightweight and framework-agnostic, and is the library The Pudding itself recommends for D3-first projects ([comparison piece](https://pudding.cool/process/how-to-implement-scrollytelling/)). This is the most proven path for a "scroll through the analysis" piece. |
| **D3 transitions (non-scroll)** | Chart-to-chart animated transitions, small multiples that animate in on load or on interaction | Low-Medium | Standard D3 idiom, no extra library needed, works everywhere. |
| **Canvas/WebGL (e.g., React Three Fiber / Three.js)** | 3D shot trajectories, dispersion clouds, physics-driven motion | High | See golf-flight.com above: real solo-dev build time was substantial, and it shipped with acknowledged rough edges (mobile layout, physics accuracy). Only worth it if the 3D-ness is the point, not for a chart that could be 2D. |
| **Lottie** | Reusing a motion-graphics animation (built in After Effects) as a lightweight web asset | Low if the animation already exists, High if it doesn't | Lottie JSON is roughly 6x smaller than the equivalent GIF and drops in via a `<dotlottie-wc>` web component ([LottieFiles guide](https://lottiefiles.com/blog/working-with-lottie-animations/how-to-add-lottie-animation-in-web-page-html)). Not a natural fit unless the piece already involves hand-built motion graphics rather than data-driven charts. |
| **Pre-rendered MP4/GIF export for social** | Turning the site's live/animated version into a LinkedIn-native asset | Low-Medium | Screen-capture the interactive (e.g., headless browser + ffmpeg) and export MP4/H.264 for LinkedIn video, or export a handful of static keyframes as carousel slides. MP4 compresses far better than GIF but can't carry transparency, so plan a solid background. Given the section-3 finding that carousels currently out-reach video on LinkedIn, prioritize keyframe/carousel export over a full video render. |

**Recommended stack for 003 specifically:** scrollama.js + D3 for the on-site scrollytelling piece (proven, documented, matches the site's existing dbt/static-site build without adding a heavy framework), with the same D3 chart states exported as a 6-9 slide static carousel for LinkedIn rather than (or in addition to) a rendered video.

## Bottom line: 2-3 combos for a solo golf-analytics creator

1. **Scrollama.js + D3 scrollytelling on-site, cut down to a static carousel for LinkedIn.** Proven pattern (NYT's Jeter piece, The Pudding's own tooling), medium effort, and it matches what's currently winning on LinkedIn (document carousels reaching 2-3x other formats per [Closely](https://blog.closelyhq.com/linkedin-video-vs-image-vs-text-algorithm-preferences-revealed/)). Exemplar: [342,000 Swings Later](https://novosmedios.gal/dsi/2015/06/07/342000-swings-later-derek-jeter-calls-it-a-career-the-new-york-times/).

2. **Animated relative small-multiple (shot/zone chart vs. tour average) with a few embedded short GIF/MP4 inserts, not full animation throughout.** Lowest effort of the three, closest to what a one-person shop can sustain weekly, and it's exactly Owen Phillips's proven model at The F5. Exemplar: [Hot Shots](https://thef5.substack.com/p/hot-shots).

3. **A D3 bar-chart-race or ranked-race format for a golf leaderboard/stat-over-time story**, rendered on-site as an SVG/D3 animation and exported as a short MP4 for LinkedIn video (accepting video's lower current LinkedIn reach in exchange for the format's proven shareability). Exemplar: [Burn-Murdoch's most-populous-cities race](https://observablehq.com/@johnburnmurdoch/bar-chart-race-the-most-populous-cities-in-the-world).
