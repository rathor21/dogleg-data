## Launch thread: hit-up and the iron flip

**Tweet 1** (182 chars) [attach the hit-up recording, or Figure 3]

TrackMan's 2010 driver chart lists 266 yards of carry at 115 mph with a -5 attack angle and 295 at +5, with the loft rising alongside. Total distance gains 31 yards on the same rows.

**Tweet 2** (210 chars) [attach Figure 3]

Hold the loft still and the carry stops climbing. In my model a Tour driver at 12.7 degrees of dynamic loft carries 285 yards near +1, 281 at +5 and 266 at +10. The spin keeps falling as the attack angle rises.

**Tweet 3** (221 chars)

The lab's driver ideal is +5 attack with loft midway between the chart's carry and total optimizers. In my model the PGA driver at that ideal carries 287 yards and totals 328, against 282 and 313 for the average delivery.

**Tweet 4** (143 chars)

The ideal uses the chart's own strike in place of the Tour spin trim. Keep the trim and the PGA carry reads 283, 1 yard over the average's 282.

**Tweet 5** (209 chars)

Irons run the other way. In my model each degree of upward attack adds 1.4 degrees of loft to an iron. From -3.9 to +3 attack, the Tour 7-iron's carry falls from 172 yards to 157 and its total from 179 to 161.

**Tweet 6** (208 chars)

The 1.4 is a modeled slope: 1.0 from swing-arc geometry, the rest from Foresight's 7-iron chart. That chart at 90 mph loses 1.7 yards of carry per degree from -6 to +2. The model, scaled to 90 mph, loses 1.8.

**Tweet 7** (277 chars with the t.co count) [attach the Day 1 recording if it did not lead the thread]

The Ball flight lab uses the same model. Set a student's miss, pin the shot, and change the path from -4 to -2: an average-amateur slice drops from 21 yards of curve to 11. Copy the link and text it to the student.

## Follow-up thread: face, path and loft

**Tweet 1** (204 chars) [attach Figure 2]

Instructors teach two rules. In my model, 83 percent of a Tour driver's start line comes from the face, and face-to-path bends a driver 2.0 times as far as a 6-iron. TrackMan's published examples say 2.3.

**Tweet 2** (196 chars) [attach the draw-versus-fade recording]

Face-to-path also changes loft. In my model a Tour driver draw (path +4, face 0) carries 267 yards and totals 322. The mirror fade carries 273 and totals 293. The draw's extra distance is run-out.

**Tweet 3** (246 chars)

The shaft leans at the lie angle, so rolling the face closed takes loft off and rolling it open adds loft: 0.61 degree per degree of face-to-path for the driver, 0.51 for the 7-iron. Draws fly lower with less spin. Fades balloon and land shorter.

**Tweet 4** (218 chars)

TrackMan's own draw-versus-fade example has 4.5 degrees of loft between the two shots. Give the model that gap and its fade spins 1,065 rpm over the draw against TrackMan's 1,125, which checks direction and rough size.

**Tweet 5** (255 chars)

The 7-iron shows it too: draw 176 carry and 185 total, fade 164 and 170. A pull hook (path -2, face -6) carries 174 and totals 182, a push slice (path +2, face +6) 163 and 169.

The coupling is a modeled assumption. A pure pull and a pure push stay equal.
